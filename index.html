Signs you need a Level 2 charger or a load check

A Level 2 charger may be right for you if your car does not fully charge overnight on a standard wall outlet, you are hunting for public chargers during the week, or a second EV is coming to the household.

A load check comes first if your home has original 100 amp service, your panel has few open breaker spaces, breakers trip when the AC and other big appliances run together, or you already run a pool pump, electric dryer, or shop circuits.

Arizona summers put heavy, long AC loads on a home, and a charger can pull steady power for hours. That combination is why we measure before we install.

How the install works

Load assessment

We look at your panel amperage, open breaker spaces, the distance from the panel to where you park, and what else you plan to add. Tell us your vehicle and the charger you want, or ask us to help you pick one.

Dedicated 240V circuit and breaker

Level 2 chargers need their own 240V circuit sized to the charger and to code. Which breaker sizes do you usually install, 50A, 60A, or both?

Hardwired or plug in outlet

Offer both, a hardwired charger or a NEMA 14 50 outlet? If you prefer one, we cut the other. Some chargers are built to be hardwired. Others can plug into a NEMA 14 50 outlet. After the site visit, we recommend the setup that fits your charger, your garage, and code.

Permit, mounting, and testing

We pull the permit, mount the charger, complete the setup steps the charger needs, and test the circuit and the charger before we leave. You get a quick walkthrough so you know how to start and stop charging.

Panel capacity and when a panel upgrade comes first

Many East Valley homes can carry a Level 2 charger. Some cannot, especially homes on original 100 amp service or homes already loaded with AC, a dryer, and pool equipment.

A Level 2 charger plus AC at the same time is a capacity question, not a guessing game. If the load check shows the panel is short, the honest first step is a panel upgrade, then the charger circuit. We handle both. A typical 200 amp panel upgrade runs $3,500 to $5,900, with Siemens or Square D equipment and a whole home surge protector included on every panel install.
